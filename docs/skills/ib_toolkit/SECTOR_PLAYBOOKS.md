# Sector-Specific Playbooks
## Adjustments to LBO/PE Analysis by Industry Vertical

*Version: 1.0.0 | Last Updated: February 2026*

---

## How to Use This Guide

When starting a new case, identify the sector and apply the relevant playbook's adjustments to the standard LBO workflow. Each playbook covers:

1. **Key Metrics** — What matters most in this sector
2. **Revenue Model** — How to build the top line
3. **Margin Drivers** — What drives profitability
4. **Leverage Considerations** — Debt capacity specifics
5. **Valuation** — Relevant multiples and benchmarks
6. **Value Creation** — Typical PE playbook for this sector
7. **Key Risks** — Sector-specific diligence focus areas
8. **Red Flags** — Warning signs specific to this industry

## 1. SaaS / Software

### Key Metrics

| Metric | What to Track | Top Quartile | Benchmark |
|--------|---------------|--------------|-----------|
| ARR | Annual Recurring Revenue | — | Primary revenue metric |
| NRR / NDR | Net Revenue Retention | >120% | Expansion vs. churn |
| GRR | Gross Revenue Retention | >90% | Logo-level stickiness |
| Rule of 40 | Growth % + EBITDA Margin % | >40% | Efficiency threshold |
| Magic Number | Net New ARR / Prior Q S&M | >0.75x | Sales efficiency |
| Burn Multiple | Net Burn / Net New ARR | <1.5x | Capital efficiency |
| CAC Payback | Months to recoup acquisition cost | <18 months | Unit economics |
| LTV:CAC | Lifetime value / acquisition cost | >3.0x | Return on S&M spend |

### Revenue Model Adjustments

**Build revenue as:**
```
Beginning ARR
+ New Logo ARR       (new customer acquisition)
+ Expansion ARR      (upsell / cross-sell / price increases)
- Contraction ARR    (downgrades)
- Churned ARR        (lost customers)
= Ending ARR

Recognized Revenue = Average ARR + Services/Other
```

**Key assumptions to stress:**
- New logo growth deceleration curve (typical: -5% per year as base grows)
- Expansion rate sustainability (driven by product roadmap, pricing power)
- Gross churn stability (watch for cohort degradation)

### Margin Drivers

| Cost Line | Typical % of Revenue | Key Driver |
|-----------|----------------------|------------|
| COGS (hosting, support) | 20-30% | Cloud infrastructure efficiency |
| S&M | 30-50% (growth stage) → 20-30% (scale) | CAC efficiency, channel mix |
| R&D | 20-30% → 15-20% | Feature velocity, AI adoption |
| G&A | 10-15% → 8-12% | Operational leverage |
| **EBITDA Margin** | **(10-25%) → 20-35%** | **Profitability inflection** |

### Leverage Considerations

- **Lower leverage typical**: 3-5x Net Debt / ARR (not EBITDA) for growth SaaS
- **Recurring revenue lending**: Banks lend against ARR, not EBITDA, for high-growth SaaS
- **Covenant focus**: ARR growth rate, NRR, cash burn rate
- **Cash conversion**: SaaS models often have strong working capital (annual prepay) but watch deferred revenue

### Valuation

| Metric | Range | When to Use |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| EV / ARR | 5-15x | Primary for growth SaaS (>30% growth) |
| EV / Revenue | 4-12x | Mixed revenue models |
| EV / EBITDA | 15-30x | Profitable SaaS at scale |
| EV / FCF | 20-40x | Mature SaaS with strong conversion |

**Multiple drivers:** Growth rate, NRR, Rule of 40, gross margin, TAM penetration

### Value Creation Playbook

1. **Pricing optimization** — Move to usage-based or value-based pricing; annual price escalators
2. **Net retention expansion** — Cross-sell additional modules; platform strategy
3. **Sales efficiency** — Optimize CAC payback; shift to product-led growth (PLG) where viable
4. **Margin expansion** — Reduce hosting costs (cloud optimization); G&A leverage at scale
5. **M&A / add-ons** — Acquire complementary products for cross-sell; consolidate fragmented verticals

### Key Risks

- **Customer concentration** — Top 10 customers >40% of ARR is high risk
- **Competitive disruption** — AI-native competitors threatening incumbents
- **NRR deterioration** — Declining expansion signals product stagnation
- **Key person risk** — Engineering talent retention
- **Platform migration risk** — Customers moving to integrated suites (e.g., Salesforce, Microsoft)

### Red Flags

- NRR trending below 100% for 2+ quarters
- Gross margin declining (infrastructure cost issues)
- Sales cycle lengthening without explanation
- Rising customer acquisition costs with flat LTV
- Multi-year contracts masking true churn (check renewal rates)

## 2. Healthcare Services

### Key Metrics

| Metric | What to Track | Benchmark |
|--------|---------------|-----------|
| Same-store growth | Revenue growth from existing facilities | 3-6% |
| Payor mix | % Medicare / Medicaid / Commercial / Self-pay | Varies by sub-sector |
| Revenue per patient/visit | Unit revenue metric | Trend stability |
| Occupancy/utilization | Facility capacity utilization | >80% target |
| Staffing ratio | Clinical staff per patient | Regulatory minimum + buffer |
| EBITDAR margin | EBITDA before rent (for leased facilities) | 15-25% |
| Days in AR | Revenue cycle efficiency | <45 days |

### Revenue Model Adjustments

**Build revenue as:**
```
Volume × Rate = Revenue

Volume drivers:
- Number of facilities/locations
- Patients per facility
- Visits per patient per year
- Same-store volume growth

Rate drivers:
- Payor mix shift
- Rate increases (Medicare annual update, commercial renegotiation)
- Acuity mix
```

**Key assumptions to stress:**
- Medicare reimbursement rate changes (annual CMS updates)
- Payor mix deterioration (shift from commercial to government)
- Same-store volume sustainability
- New facility ramp-up curves (typically 18-36 months to stabilize)

### Margin Drivers

| Cost Line | Typical % | Key Driver |
|-----------|-----------|------------|
| Labor (clinical) | 40-55% | Staffing ratios, wage inflation |
| Labor (admin) | 8-12% | Back-office scale |
| Supplies | 10-20% | GPO contracts, utilization |
| Rent/occupancy | 5-10% | Lease vs. own, facility age |
| **EBITDA Margin** | **12-22%** | **Operating leverage, payor mix** |

### Leverage Considerations

- **Moderate leverage**: 4-6x Debt/EBITDA standard
- **Recurring revenue**: Medicare/Medicaid payments are quasi-contractual
- **Covenant focus**: Fixed charge coverage, leverage ratio, minimum liquidity
- **EBITDAR analysis**: Lenders adjust for rent expense (critical for leased-facility businesses)
- **Watch**: Labor cost inflation can rapidly compress margins

### Valuation

| Metric | Range | Notes |
|--------|-------|-------|
| EV / EBITDA | 10-16x | Most common |
| EV / Revenue | 1.5-4x | Lower margin businesses |
| EV / Bed or EV / Facility | Varies | Capacity-based valuation |

### Value Creation Playbook

1. **De novo expansion** — Open new facilities in underserved markets
2. **Tuck-in acquisitions** — Roll up fragmented local operators
3. **Revenue cycle optimization** — Reduce days in AR, improve coding accuracy
4. **Labor management** — Optimize staffing models, reduce agency/temp reliance
5. **Payor mix improvement** — Shift toward higher-reimbursement commercial contracts
6. **Technology/automation** — EMR optimization, telehealth integration, AI-assisted coding

### Key Risks

- **Reimbursement cuts** — CMS rate reductions, state Medicaid budget pressure
- **Regulatory / compliance** — Stark Law, Anti-Kickback, state licensing
- **Labor shortages** — Nursing/clinical staff shortages, wage inflation
- **Litigation** — Malpractice, False Claims Act (qui tam), class action
- **Concentration** — Single-payor or single-geography dependence

### Red Flags

- Payor mix shifting toward Medicaid without rate increases
- Rising days in AR (billing/collection problems)
- High agency staffing costs (>15% of labor = structural problem)
- Declining same-store volumes without clear cause
- Frequent state survey deficiencies or CMS sanctions

## 3. Industrial / Manufacturing

### Key Metrics

| Metric | What to Track | Benchmark |
|--------|---------------|-----------|
| Capacity utilization | % of max production capacity | >75% target |
| Backlog / book-to-bill | Order pipeline vs. shipments | >1.0x healthy |
| OEE | Overall Equipment Effectiveness | >85% world-class |
| Gross margin by product line | Product mix profitability | Varies |
| Working capital intensity | NWC as % of revenue | 15-25% typical |
| CapEx intensity | CapEx as % of revenue | 3-8% maintenance; 10-15% growth |
| Revenue per employee | Productivity metric | Trend improvement |

### Revenue Model Adjustments

**Build revenue as:**
```
Product-level approach:
  Units × Price = Revenue per Product Line
  Sum across product lines = Total Revenue

Order-based approach:
  Opening Backlog
  + New Orders (book-to-bill × prior shipments)
  - Shipments/Revenue
  = Closing Backlog
```

**Key assumptions to stress:**
- Cyclical demand patterns (tied to GDP, housing, industrial production)
- Input cost pass-through (raw materials, energy)
- Capacity constraints and expansion timing
- Customer concentration and contract terms

### Margin Drivers

| Cost Line | Typical % | Key Driver |
|-----------|-----------|------------|
| Raw materials | 30-50% | Commodity prices, supplier contracts |
| Direct labor | 15-25% | Wage rates, automation |
| Overhead / utilities | 10-15% | Fixed cost leverage |
| SG&A | 8-15% | Sales force, distribution |
| **EBITDA Margin** | **12-22%** | **Volume leverage, mix, pass-through** |

### Leverage Considerations

- **Conservative leverage**: 3-5x Debt/EBITDA (cyclicality demands lower leverage)
- **Asset-backed lending**: Can use ABL facility secured by inventory + AR
- **Covenant focus**: Fixed charge coverage, leverage ratio, minimum EBITDA
- **Working capital facility**: Seasonal businesses may need WC revolvers
- **Cyclicality buffer**: Lenders stress for downturn (20-30% EBITDA decline)

### Valuation

| Metric | Range | Notes |
|--------|-------|-------|
| EV / EBITDA | 6-10x | Most common; cycle-adjust |
| EV / Revenue | 1.0-2.5x | Lower margin industrials |
| EV / Ton or EV / Unit capacity | Varies | Replacement cost analysis |

**Critical:** Use mid-cycle EBITDA for entry valuation, not peak or trough.

### Value Creation Playbook

1. **Operational efficiency** — Lean manufacturing, OEE improvement, waste reduction
2. **Procurement savings** — Consolidate suppliers, renegotiate raw material contracts
3. **Automation / CapEx** — Invest in automation to reduce labor dependency
4. **Product mix optimization** — Shift toward higher-margin products and services
5. **Geographic expansion** — Enter new markets / nearshoring trends
6. **Bolt-on acquisitions** — Add capabilities, customer relationships, or capacity

### Key Risks

- **Cyclicality** — Revenue tied to economic cycle; model downside scenarios carefully
- **Input cost volatility** — Commodity price spikes compressing margins
- **Customer concentration** — Single large OEM customer = high risk
- **Environmental / regulatory** — EPA compliance, emissions, waste disposal liabilities
- **CapEx requirements** — Aging equipment creates step-function CapEx needs
- **Foreign competition** — Import substitution, tariff changes

### Red Flags

- Backlog declining for 2+ consecutive quarters
- Capacity utilization below 70% for extended period
- Working capital growing faster than revenue
- Deferred maintenance CapEx creating safety or reliability risk
- Single customer >25% of revenue

## 4. Business Services

### Key Metrics

| Metric | What to Track | Benchmark |
|--------|---------------|-----------|
| Recurring / contracted revenue % | Revenue visibility | >70% strong |
| Client retention rate | Logo-level retention | >90% target |
| Revenue per FTE | Productivity / leverage | Trend improvement |
| Organic vs. acquired growth | Quality of growth | Organic >5% preferred |
| Utilization rate | Billable hours / total hours | >75% for staffing |
| Average contract value | Deal size trends | Growing = healthy |
| Same-client growth | Wallet share expansion | >5% target |

### Revenue Model Adjustments

**Build revenue as:**
```
Contracted / Recurring:
  Opening Contracted Revenue
  + New Contracts Won
  + Contract Renewals with Price Escalators
  - Contract Losses / Non-Renewals
  = Ending Contracted Revenue

Non-Recurring / Project:
  Pipeline × Win Rate × Average Deal Size = Project Revenue
```

**Key assumptions to stress:**
- Contract renewal rates and pricing power
- New client acquisition pace
- Revenue per FTE trends (wage inflation vs. bill rate increases)
- M&A integration and revenue synergy realization

### Margin Drivers

| Cost Line | Typical % | Key Driver |
|-----------|-----------|------------|
| Direct labor / subcontractors | 50-65% | Bill rate vs. pay rate spread |
| Selling expenses | 5-10% | Sales force, commissions |
| Corporate / G&A | 8-15% | Shared services leverage |
| **EBITDA Margin** | **15-25%** | **Utilization, leverage, retention** |

### Leverage Considerations

- **Moderate leverage**: 4-6x Debt/EBITDA
- **Asset-light**: High FCF conversion supports higher leverage
- **Covenant focus**: Leverage ratio, fixed charge coverage
- **M&A capacity**: Business services are prime rollup candidates; maintain acquisition capacity
- **Earnout structures**: Common for tuck-in acquisitions (talent retention)

### Valuation

| Metric | Range | Notes |
|--------|-------|-------|
| EV / EBITDA | 8-14x | Platform vs. add-on premium |
| EV / Revenue | 1.5-3.5x | Depends on margin and growth |

### Value Creation Playbook

1. **Buy-and-build** — Roll up fragmented market; create platform
2. **Cross-sell services** — Expand wallet share with existing clients
3. **Pricing optimization** — Annual escalators, value-based pricing, reduce discounting
4. **Offshore / nearshore leverage** — Move delivery to lower-cost geographies
5. **Technology enablement** — Automate service delivery, improve margins
6. **Contract conversion** — Shift project-based to recurring / retainer models

### Key Risks

- **Key person risk** — Founder/partner relationships drive revenue
- **Client concentration** — Top 5 clients >40% of revenue
- **Talent retention** — Service businesses = people businesses
- **Organic growth stall** — Growth only from acquisitions is unsustainable
- **Integration risk** — Acquiring and retaining acquired talent

### Red Flags

- Client retention falling below 85%
- Revenue per FTE declining (margin squeeze)
- High employee turnover (>25% annually)
- Growth entirely acquisition-driven with no organic
- Utilization below 70% for billable staff

## 5. Consumer / Retail

### Key Metrics

| Metric | What to Track | Benchmark |
|--------|---------------|-----------|
| Same-store sales (SSS) | Organic growth at existing locations | >3% healthy |
| Unit-level economics | 4-wall EBITDA, contribution margin | Varies by format |
| Customer acquisition cost | Cost per new customer | Declining trend |
| Average order value (AOV) | Revenue per transaction | Growing = healthy |
| Store-level payback | Months to recoup new store investment | <24 months |
| Inventory turns | Efficiency of inventory management | 4-8x for most retail |
| Gross margin | Pricing power and sourcing | Category-dependent |

### Revenue Model Adjustments

**Build revenue as (Physical Retail):**
```
Existing Stores:
  Prior Year Revenue × (1 + Same-Store Sales Growth) = Existing Store Revenue

New Stores:
  # New Store Openings × Ramp Revenue Curve = New Store Revenue

Total Revenue = Existing + New Store Revenue

Ramp Curve (typical):
  Year 1: 60-70% of mature revenue
  Year 2: 85-90%
  Year 3: 100% (mature)
```

**Build revenue as (DTC / E-Commerce):**
```
Site Traffic × Conversion Rate × AOV = Revenue
Customer Count × Purchases per Year × AOV = Revenue
```

### Margin Drivers

| Cost Line | Typical % | Key Driver |
|-----------|-----------|------------|
| COGS / product cost | 40-65% | Sourcing, private label %, mix |
| Rent / occupancy | 8-15% | Lease negotiations, format optimization |
| Labor (store-level) | 12-20% | Min wage exposure, productivity |
| Marketing | 5-15% | CAC efficiency, channel mix |
| **EBITDA Margin** | **10-20%** | **SSS leverage, format optimization** |

### Leverage Considerations

- **Conservative leverage**: 3-5x Debt/EBITDA (consumer discretionary volatility)
- **Lease-adjusted leverage**: EBITDAR analysis critical (capitalize operating leases)
- **Seasonal WC**: Inventory build ahead of peak season requires revolver
- **Covenant focus**: Fixed charge coverage (including rent), leverage ratio
- **ABL facility**: Secured by inventory + AR for working capital needs

### Valuation

| Metric | Range | Notes |
|--------|-------|-------|
| EV / EBITDA | 7-12x | Premium for high-growth brands |
| EV / EBITDAR | 6-10x | Adjusts for lease structure |
| EV / Revenue | 0.5-2.0x | Low-margin businesses |
| EV / Store | Varies | Unit economics valuation |

### Value Creation Playbook

1. **New unit growth** — Expand store count in proven format
2. **Same-store sales** — Menu/assortment optimization, CRM, loyalty programs
3. **Private label / owned brands** — Higher margin product mix
4. **Omnichannel development** — Integrate online + offline
5. **Supply chain optimization** — Reduce COGS through direct sourcing, logistics
6. **Format innovation** — Smaller formats, new concepts, international expansion

### Key Risks

- **Consumer discretionary sensitivity** — Revenue tied to consumer confidence/spending
- **Real estate risk** — Long-term leases, location quality deterioration
- **E-commerce disruption** — Shift to online reducing foot traffic
- **Commodity / COGS inflation** — Input costs rise faster than price increases
- **Brand fatigue** — Consumer preferences shift rapidly

### Red Flags

- Negative same-store sales for 2+ consecutive quarters
- New store payback period lengthening
- Inventory turns declining (overstock / stale merchandise)
- Marketing spend rising with flat customer counts
- Lease expiry concentration in near term

## Sector Selection Quick Reference

| If the target is... | Use Playbook | Primary Multiple | Key Diligence Focus |
|---------------------|--------------|------------------|---------------------|
| Cloud software company | SaaS / Software | EV/ARR | NRR, churn cohorts, competitive moat |
| Hospital / clinic / home health | Healthcare Services | EV/EBITDA | Payor mix, reimbursement, regulatory |
| Manufacturer / distributor | Industrial | EV/EBITDA (mid-cycle) | Cyclicality, working capital, CapEx |
| Consulting / staffing / outsourcing | Business Services | EV/EBITDA | Retention, organic growth, key person |
| Retailer / restaurant / brand | Consumer / Retail | EV/EBITDA or EBITDAR | SSS, unit economics, lease exposure |
