## IC Presentation Flow & Timing

### Standard IC Deck Walkthrough (15-20 Minutes)

| Section | Slides | Time | What to Say | What NOT to Say |
|---------|--------|------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Opening** | Title + Exec Summary | 2 min | Lead with recommendation and key numbers. "We recommend a Buy at X.Xx — here's why." | Don't read the slide. Don't apologize for time constraints. |
| **Company Story** | Overview | 1.5 min | What they do, for whom, how they make money. One sentence on founding story if compelling. | Don't recite the full history. Skip funding rounds unless relevant. |
| **Why This Deal** | Thesis (3 pillars) | 2 min | State each pillar as a claim, give one data point per pillar, explain the "so what" for returns. | Don't give 3 paragraphs per pillar. Don't use marketing language. |
| **Market Opportunity** | TAM/SAM | 1.5 min | Focus on "why now" — what's changing. Give the addressable market number with conviction. | Don't over-explain TAM methodology. Don't present aspirational TAM. |
| **Competition** | Positioning | 1.5 min | Name the 2-3 real competitors. Explain why target wins. Use win rate data if available. | Don't dismiss competition. Don't claim "no competitors." |
| **Financials** | P&L Overview | 2 min | Revenue trajectory, margin path, key inflection. Point to the historical/projected transition. | Don't read every line item. Don't explain standard accounting. |
| **Returns** | MOIC/IRR + Sensitivity | 2.5 min | State base case returns. Walk through the sensitivity — what drives upside and downside. | Don't only present base case. Don't skip sensitivity discussion. |
| **Risks** | Risk Matrix + WMBT | 2 min | "Here are the 3 things that keep me up at night, and here's how we mitigate each." | Don't bury risks. Don't present 10 risks superficially. |
| **Recommendation** | Final Slide | 1 min | Restate recommendation, key condition (if qualified buy), and proposed next steps. | Don't introduce new information. Don't waffle on recommendation. |

### Pacing Tips

- **Talk to the IC, not the screen** — Slides are reference material, not a script
- **One message per slide** — State the slide's key insight in your first sentence, then support it
- **Leave 5 minutes for Q&A** — A 20-minute slot means 15 minutes of presentation
- **Pause after key numbers** — Let "$3.2 billion TAM growing at 18%" land before moving on
- **Use transitions** — "Now that we've established the market opportunity, let me show you why this company wins..."

## Handling Objections Framework

### The ARIA Framework

When an IC member pushes back on your thesis:

1. **Acknowledge** — "That's a fair concern" (never be defensive)
2. **Reframe** — Put the objection in context
3. **Address with data** — Use a specific number or fact
4. **Implication** — Explain what it means for returns

### Common Objections and Responses

**"The valuation is too high"**
```
Acknowledge: "Valuation is clearly at a premium to peer medians."
Reframe:     "But when we adjust for growth rate and retention quality..."
Data:        "At 12x NTM revenue, we're paying [X]x on a PEG-adjusted basis,
             vs. peer median of [Y]x. The premium is [Z]% for [A]% faster growth."
Implication: "Even if we compress the exit multiple by 2 turns,
             we still achieve [X]x MOIC based on growth alone."
```

**"What if growth slows?"**
```
Acknowledge: "Growth deceleration is the biggest assumption in our model."
Reframe:     "The question is how much deceleration, not whether."
Data:        "In our bear case, growth slows to [X]% (vs. [Y]% base),
             and we still achieve [X]x MOIC / [X]% IRR."
Implication: "The downside is [X]x — we get our money back even in the
             worst realistic scenario. The asymmetry is attractive."
```

**"The competition will crush them"**
```
Acknowledge: "[Competitor] is a serious player with [X] market share."
Reframe:     "But they're solving a different problem for a different customer."
Data:        "Win rate vs. [Competitor] is [X]% based on [X] head-to-head deals.
             ICP overlap is only [X]%."
Implication: "The market is large enough for both. Our thesis doesn't require
             winning against [Competitor] — just winning our segment."
```

**"What's your edge? Why haven't other funds done this?"**
```
Acknowledge: "It's a competitive process."
Reframe:     "Our edge comes from [sector expertise / operating partner / timing]."
Data:        "We've diligenced [X] companies in this space over [X] months.
             Our operating partner [Name] built [X] to [Y] revenue at [Company]."
Implication: "We have conviction based on proprietary diligence that others
             may not have completed."
```

**"Management team is unproven"**
```
Acknowledge: "The team is early in their journey together."
Reframe:     "But individually, the experience is relevant."
Data:        "The CEO scaled [prior company] from $[X]M to $[Y]M ARR.
             The CTO built [X technology] at [Company]."
Implication: "We've budgeted for a VP Sales hire in Year 1 and have
             3 candidates identified through our network."
```

## Verbal Presentation Tips

### Structure Every Answer

Use the **Headline → Support → Implication** pattern:

```
"The short answer is [headline].

This is because [supporting data point 1] and [supporting data point 2].

What this means for returns is [implication]."
```

### Things to Say

- "In our base case..." (grounds the discussion)
- "The data suggests..." (evidence-based)
- "The key risk here is X, and our mitigation is Y" (proactive risk management)
- "Relative to peers, this company..." (benchmarked perspective)
- "If we stress-test that assumption..." (shows you've done the work)

### Things to Avoid

- "I think..." (sounds uncertain — use "the analysis shows" or "we believe")
- "It's really exciting" (sounds promotional — let the numbers speak)
- "We couldn't find data on..." (sounds unprepared — say "we're diligencing X through Y approach")
- "Just to be safe, we assumed..." (sounds like sandbagging — explain why the assumption is appropriate)
- Reading numbers from the slide (the IC can read — add interpretation)

### Body Language

- **Face the IC, not the screen** — Glance at the slide, then talk to the room
- **Pause before answering tough questions** — 2-3 seconds of thought shows confidence
- **Don't rush through risks** — Spending time on risks shows maturity
- **Make eye contact with the questioner** — Direct your answer to who asked

## Q&A Preparation Template

Before any IC presentation, prepare written answers for these 15 questions:

### Must-Prepare (The IC Will Ask These)

1. "Walk me through the thesis in 60 seconds."
2. "What's the biggest risk to this investment?"
3. "Why this company at this price?"
4. "What does the downside look like?"
5. "Who buys this at exit and why?"

### Likely to Be Asked

6. "How does this compare to [competitor/peer]?"
7. "What are the first 100 days post-close?"
8. "Where do the returns come from? (Bridge the value creation.)"
9. "What would make you walk away?"
10. "What's the quality of the management team?"

### Stress-Test Questions

11. "What if growth is 50% of your base case?"
12. "What if the exit multiple compresses by 2 turns?"
13. "What if the key customer churns?"
14. "What if interest rates rise 200 bps?"
15. "What's the covenant break scenario?"

### Answer Format for Each

```
Question: [Question]

Headline Answer (1 sentence):
[Direct answer]

Supporting Data (2-3 points):
1. [Data point with source]
2. [Data point with source]
3. [Data point with source]

Impact on Returns:
[How this affects MOIC/IRR in base and stress cases]

Backup Slide Reference:
[Slide # or appendix reference]
```

## Presentation Checklist (Day Before)

- [ ] Can I present the thesis in 60 seconds without notes?
- [ ] Do I know the 5 key numbers by heart (IRR, MOIC, entry multiple, exit multiple, revenue CAGR)?
- [ ] Have I prepared written answers for all 15 Q&A questions above?
- [ ] Have I rehearsed the full presentation at least once (under 15 minutes)?
- [ ] Do I know which slides to skip if I'm running long?
- [ ] Have I identified 3 slides I can deep-dive on if the IC wants more detail?
- [ ] Is there a backup appendix for detailed questions?
- [ ] Do I have the model open and ready for live scenario changes?
